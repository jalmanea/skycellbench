# Evaluation protocol

**Status: frozen on 2026-11-28**, at the commit that made this line. Every held-out result
records the hash of that commit. From here on a change to anything in this file, or to a
controller, prompt, hyperparameter or scorer setting, is recorded under section 8 with its
reason, and all controllers are rerun.

## 1. What is evaluated

A controller receives the public copy of an instance and returns one position `(x, y, altitude)`
per UAV. The three tasks, the rules and the score are those of `README.md` at the frozen commit:

- `place`: positions only.
- `constrained`: no-fly zones and a backhaul chain to a gateway.
- `blind`: user positions hidden; information comes only from probes.

Score: mean over users of `min(1, delivered throughput / demand)`. An answer that breaks a rule
scores 0 for that instance and is counted as invalid in the report.

## 2. Instances

- Held-out set: `instances/heldout`, generated by `experiments/make_sets.py` with seeds
  200000 to 200029, 30 instances per task (90 in all). It is used once, for the reported
  results.
- Public set: `instances/public`, seeds 100000 to 100009, 10 instances per task. All
  development, tuning and training checks use this set or freshly generated instances with
  seeds below 90000.
- Both sets stand on flat ground with the statistical channel. `instances/public-cincinnati-downtown`
  and `instances/heldout-cincinnati-downtown` hold the same seeds on the Cincinnati scene
  (`README.md`, Scenes), under the same rules as the flat sets: the public one is for
  development, the held-out one is not scored before freezing.
- Results are reported on both grounds, flat and the scene, as two separate tables. Scores are
  not averaged across the two.
- `tests/test_scene.py` regenerates every set from its seeds and checks it against the files,
  without scoring anything.
- No controller, prompt, hyperparameter or scorer setting may be changed after any held-out
  instance has been scored. If one must change, the change and its reason are recorded under
  section 8, and all controllers are rerun.

## 3. Scorers

- Reported score: ns-3.48 LTE (`score_ns3`), settings as in `README.md`, 2 s measured, run
  number 1.
- Both scorers take the path loss of every link from one table computed by `fast.path_loss_db`.
- The closed-form scorer (`score_fast`) is what controllers may call while they work. Its score
  on the final answer is reported beside the ns-3 score, never in its place.
- A controller never calls ns-3.

## 4. Budgets

The budget is the number of scorer calls a controller may make on one instance, counting every
call to `score_fast` or to the probe, including calls on invalid placements.

| Track | Budget | Tasks |
|---|---|---|
| Low | 30 calls | `place`, `constrained`, `blind` |
| High | 4,000 calls | `place`, `constrained` |

- On `blind` the probe is the only scorer and its budget is 30, so `blind` has no high track.
- On `place` and `constrained` the controller also sees the users, so it may compute anything
  from the instance itself without spending budget, as long as it does not reimplement the
  scorer.
- Cost outside the budget is reported for each controller: training steps for learned policies;
  model, number of model calls and tokens for LLM controllers.

## 5. Controllers

| Controller | Low track | High track |
|---|---|---|
| Do-nothing | yes | (same answer) |
| Random valid placement | yes | (same answer) |
| k-means, best of 3 altitudes | yes (3 calls) | (same answer) |
| Local search | 30 calls | 4,000 calls |
| RL (PPO, one policy per task) | one rollout, 29 calls | not entered |
| LLM agent | added later, see below | added later, see below |

- RL: on flat ground the policy trained on flat ground (`rl`, `experiments/models/rl-<task>.pt`);
  on the scene the policy trained on the scene (`rl_scene`, `rl-<task>-cincinnati-downtown.pt`),
  600,000 steps each, seed 0. Both are entered as they are now and not retrained again. The
  flat-trained policy on the scene is reported beside it as a transfer check, not as an entry.
  The weight files are not in the repository; the entered ones have these SHA-256 hashes:
  - `rl-place.pt` b5a2e64dd3fc973c2bdf1677a8e67080a37eaefcf8bb06424c81384b55b92d72
  - `rl-constrained.pt` fd0e04d6c0f9b56d261b92e8ef98abeb440985c07d6c825172bdb7b97f759488
  - `rl-blind.pt` 8105176994abbd6d9a239bdd7f112c49c5860ae75df2dc4652851e7e1de95fa8
  - `rl-place-cincinnati-downtown.pt` f9084158aad67c8b01fbea0657ff5858809fd6a6a6ef33dd4e9fb16b4e348f2e
  - `rl-constrained-cincinnati-downtown.pt` 518d5e3591d6788d890c75efcca0c7367de186a0c715052955af0a02121279ff

- Controllers that use random numbers (random, local search) are run with 3 seeds per instance;
  the instance score is the mean of the 3. Deterministic controllers are run once.
- The reference (200,000-call search with full knowledge) is reported as a level, not as a
  controller.

- LLM agent: not part of the first held-out run. One LLM controller may be added later and run
  once on the same held-out instances, under these rules fixed now:
  - It is defined (model and version, prompt, tools, number of model calls, token limit, the
    tracks it enters) in a dated addition to this section, committed before it sees any
    held-out instance.
  - It is developed on the public sets only. After its held-out run nothing about it is changed;
    a second version is a second controller with its own addition, and both are reported.
  - It gets the same budgets, the same scorer calls and the same public copy of each instance
    as every other controller, and its cost (calls, tokens) is reported as in section 4.
  - Its results are published with the date of its run, beside the first run, which is not
    rerun or changed.

## 6. Statistics

- Per task and track: mean ns-3 score over the held-out instances, with a 95% bootstrap interval
  over instances (10,000 resamples).
- Comparisons between two controllers are paired by instance: mean difference with a 95%
  bootstrap interval, and the number of instances won.
- No claim that one controller is better than another is made when the interval of the paired
  difference includes 0.
- Reported per controller beside the score: invalid answers, scorer calls used, sum throughput,
  5th-percentile user, wall time.

## 7. What is published

The instances, every answer (positions), both scores per answer, the scripts, and this file.

## 8. Deviations

None yet.

## Checked on the public set

- Scorer agreement: 170 answers, Spearman 0.98 to 0.99 per task; the closed-form score is higher
  than ns-3 by 0.04 to 0.06 on average and never lower; ns-3 keeps the order of 387 of 397
  controller pairs.
- ns-3 run-to-run variation: 15 answers with 4 run numbers each; the standard deviation of the
  score is 0.0015 on average and 0.005 at most, so one run per answer is enough.

- On the Cincinnati scene (`instances/public-cincinnati-downtown`): 200 answers from seven
  controllers (the scene-trained RL policy included), Spearman 0.99 to 1.00 per task; the
  closed-form score is higher than ns-3 by 0.03 to 0.05 on average, by 0.17 at most, and lower
  for one answer (by 0.003); ns-3 keeps the order of 559 of 567 controller pairs.

## Settled

- **Held-out size (2026-11-28):** 30 instances per task on each ground, 180 in all. With 10, a
  paired difference had a 95% interval about 0.13 wide on the public scene set; 30 should make it
  about 40% narrower. Both held-out sets were regenerated with the extra seeds before any of them
  was scored; the 30 instances of the first ten seeds are the same as before.
- **LLM controller (2026-11-28):** not in the first held-out run; added later under the rules
  of section 5.
- **Grounds (2026-11-28):** both, flat and the Cincinnati scene (section 2).
- **RL entry (2026-11-28):** the current policies, one per task and ground (section 5). The
  scene-trained policy is better than the flat-trained one only on `constrained` (+0.075, 95%
  interval +0.012 to +0.147, public scene set); it is entered anyway because it is the policy
  trained for that ground.
- **No moving task (2026-11-28).** A task with moving users would not test what it is meant to
  test, planning ahead. On the scene, moving UAVs gain up to 0.115 over fixed ones, but UAVs that
  only react to the current epoch get all of that except 0.005 at most on average (0.017 on any
  instance): knowing the future adds almost nothing, so the task would be `place` repeated per
  epoch. On flat ground moving is worth less still: free movement gains 0.02 to 0.06. It also
  needs three times the normal demand to show anything. Details in `README.md`, "A fourth task, `moving`, was removed". The
  instance format and the scorers still accept one placement per epoch.
